# Two-year thesis

Within two years, consequential AI actions will stop flowing directly from model output to tools. A decision layer will become a standard transaction boundary between probabilistic agents and deterministic systems of record.

The important shift is not better confidence scoring. Decision layers will own the evidence snapshot, policy version, risk posture, reversibility, required approvals, and execution outcome for every material action. An agent may propose a refund, deployment, account change, or message; the decision layer will determine whether that proposal can execute now, needs a fact, should wait for a changing state, must move to a higher authority, or should never run.

These layers will become feedback systems. Outcomes will attach to the original decision record, letting teams measure false allows, unnecessary escalations, stale evidence, and domain-specific failure cost. Policies will evolve from static thresholds into versioned, testable controls informed by real outcomes, while the final authority boundary remains outside the model.

The winning architecture will be model-agnostic and tool-agnostic. Companies will not trust an agent because it is smart. They will trust a system because every consequential action has a reproducible decision, an explicit authority path, and an auditable record of what was known when the action was allowed.
